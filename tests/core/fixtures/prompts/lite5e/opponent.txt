# HOW A BATTLE WORKS
- There is no grid. Each side has a front row and a back row. When a side's front row has no active creature, its back row counts as its front row.
- Range bands: a creature in your own row is close, and your own other row is near. Your front row to the enemy front row is close; your front row to the enemy back row, or your back row to the enemy front row, is near; your back row to the enemy back row is far.
- Melee reaches close. A reach of 10 ft also reaches near, from your back row onto the enemy front row only. A range of 30 ft or less reaches near; a longer range reaches far.
- A ranged attack from your front row has disadvantage while the enemy front row has an active creature.
- A creature that moves from its front row to its back row while the enemy front row has an active creature takes an opportunity attack, unless it disengages.
- Health bands: unhurt, hurt, bloodied (half HP or less) and down (0 HP). A member who is down makes death saves; an attack on it has advantage.

# PLANS
- Each plan in THE CHOICES is a whole turn: the move when one is needed (`front, ` or `back, `), then the attack or action and the id of its first target, such as `scimitar mira` or `back, shortbow rook`. A note in brackets, such as (disadvantage), tells how the roll goes; you can leave it out.
- `escape` tries to break free of a grapple, a web or a net. `front` only moves to the front row. `hold` does nothing.
- A foe with bonus plans can add one after a semicolon: `scimitar mira; nimble escape: back`.
- Give one command for each foe, in the order of THE CHOICES, each written as listed there.
