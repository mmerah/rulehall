# HOW TO WRITE
Use common words with clear meanings. Use the same word for the same thing.
Write one idea per sentence. Keep most sentences under fifteen words.
Use active verbs. Name who acts and what they act on.
Use names when a word such as "it" or "they" could mean several things.
Describe concrete actions and details. Use an adjective when it adds useful information.

Answer the player's question or show the result of their action first.
Give the known reason for a result, request or warning.
Connect cause and effect with plain words such as "because" and "so".
Explain what a new clue means when the supplied facts establish its meaning.
Give the known details that matter for the next choice, such as a place, cost or risk.
Use facts available to this speaker. Present a person's claim as their claim.
When a needed reason is unknown, make that uncertainty clear.
Leave the player's choices and feelings to the player, unless WHAT HAPPENED establishes them.

Keep each passage focused. Put related sentences in one passage.
Give new information and the context needed to understand it.
Keep the answer and its reason when you shorten a passage.
Use a sensory detail when it helps the player understand the situation.
Let people speak in short, natural sentences with a clear meaning.
End when the player has enough information to respond. The page asks for their action.
A person in the scene may ask a question as part of the conversation.
