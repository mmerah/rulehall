# Loner 4e

A solo oracle-driven system: Chance d6 against Risk d6, six outcomes, a Twist Counter, Harm resolved against Luck.

## The tools

The game master has eight Loner tools besides the shared `enter`, `leave`, `kill`, `join_party`, `leave_party` and `direct`; a normal turn is one `ask` and `direct`. Every tool makes one change, rolls dice, opens a decision, or ends the turn.

- `ask` — one closed question for the protagonist, Chance against Risk. The game master cites the tags that bear on it (`helps`, `hinders`, where `Untrained` stands for missing expertise); the engine checks each one exists here, whichever side it is cited on (reading 1), and nets them into one extra die at most. A single tag sent bare, where a list is asked, reads as that list of one. The answer goes into SETTLED THIS SCENE, and the game master's result carries the SRD's outcome line beside it ("You succeed in your action", "You fail + something makes it worse"), so a plain yes is not read as a yes, and; the player's own question (**Ask**) carries the answer only, since its yes may not be what the protagonist hopes. Doubles outside a conflict count on the Twist Counter, and the card says "+1 Twist (n/3)"; the third rolls a twist, and "Ends the scene" closes the scene in the same call. `opponent_id` makes the question a Harm & Luck exchange: the first one opens the conflict and refills both sides, a yes costs the opponent luck and a no costs the protagonist. While a conflict is open, every `ask` is an exchange (reading 29): with no `opponent_id`, against the opponent fought last (the last one an exchange or **Fight** faced); the game master calls `withdraw` first when the protagonist turns to anything else. CONFLICT lists each opponent's skills and gear as `hinders`, so an exchange cites the opponent's side too. An `opponent_id` that names no one in the cast brings in the stranger the story just named: code files a character with 6 luck under that id (`dock-guard` reads "Dock Guard") and enters them, and the result lists the rest of the cast by name and id, so a duplicate shows ("Use one of those ids when you mean them"); one who is in the cast but elsewhere enters. The player's question with no conflict open is a plain question even when the game master sends an `opponent_id`: it asks what is true, and opens no exchange (the round 6 test charged a fact question as an exchange). With no player question waiting, a missing `question` is refused with "`question` is required: write the question". A dead end is an `ask` of the SRD's "Does something or someone point toward a new way forward?", read by `rules.md`; a second one in the same scene is refused with "close the scene as `blocked`" (reading 21). The SRD's last-resort "Has this scene run its course?" is an `ask` too: on any yes, code closes the scene as `ran_its_course` and rolls the transition.
- `roll_inspiration` — the SRD's inspiration tables for an open question (also the player's **Inspire**): code rolls 2d6 each for a verb, an adjective and a noun, the card shows the three words, and the game master reads them into what is already here.
- `withdraw` — the protagonist breaks off a conflict (also the player's **Break away**); the game master's result says to name the cost with `change_tags`.
- `change_tags` (a character's tags, a `relationship` on an NPC, or with `actor_id: scene` the scene's `detail` tags, where `kind` may be left out; past six details, the oldest give way with no card, so stale beats leave the scene), `drive` (goal, motive, nemesis; in the growth, the protagonist's `concept` too; with `actor_id: scene` the scene's goal) and `spend_luck` (pay a cost the played pack's special rules print, such as an AP01 spell; the tool is published only when the game's pack spends luck, since SPECIAL RULES come from that pack alone). `enter` and `change_tags` treat an id as `opponent_id` does: `crane` names a lone `silas-crane`, someone in the cast who is elsewhere enters, and any other new id files a stranger under it and enters them; `enter` of someone already here and `leave` of someone not here or of a body change nothing. A lost tag matches in any case and in any kind (`Bearded Axe` drops `Bearded axe`; a `condition` named as lost comes off the frailties when it is one). A new skill or frailty on the protagonist is the growth: `change_tags` refuses it until the player picks **End it**, and then takes one (reading 30). After **End it**, `drive` on the protagonist is played even though the scene has closed, since the usual end closes the scene first, and a `concept` is taken there only; a new concept or nemesis shows on a card, as a goal does.
- `close_scene` — the scene's goal is resolved, blocked or abandoned, or a quiet scene reaches its `turning_point`. A `turning_point` in a dramatic scene is refused with the three reasons that close one. A Quiet scene is never closed as `resolved` or `turning_point` on the player's first turn in it (reading 26). A `resolved` or `abandoned` close's result adds "If this settles what the protagonist set out to do, call `end_adventure` now." (reading 27). Once the scene has closed, `ask`, `drive`, `enter`, `leave` and a scene `detail` are refused, each refusal saying to call `direct` now: only `direct` is left. The engine rolls the Scene Transition, and after the turn the worldsmith writes a Dramatic scene, or the page offers the player **Take the breather**: their words are the aim of a Quiet scene. On a 6 (Meanwhile) the engine asks the SRD's "Does an ally or wildcard act independently?", keeps the answer for the worldsmith rather than in SETTLED THIS SCENE, and rolls the follow-up (reading 41; a second 6 reads as Dramatic, deviation 4); the worldsmith updates the tags of whoever holds power (`power`; between scenes everyone but the party is off screen, the people of the scene just left too) and, on a yes only, of the NPC most affected (`ally`: code refuses one on a no), the player reads what moved on one cutaway card, and the Dramatic scene or the breather follows. A `turning_point` rolls no transition: the Dramatic scene opens on the player's action that tipped the quiet scene, still unresolved.
- `end_adventure` — proposes the end when one of the SRD's signs shows (its description lists them: the goal achieved or definitively failed, the goal abandoned with no new direction, a major revelation, a satisfying resolution, the momentum slowed); the player picks **End it** or **Play on**, and words cannot answer it. **End it** asks "What did {name} learn?"; the player's words answer it, and on that turn only the game master reads THE END IS PROPOSED and writes the growth once, the worldsmith writes the Living World (what became of the people, places and events; each person is sent as an `entity_id` of someone the player met and a line, and code writes their name, so no id reaches the card), the player reads it as the closing card, and the game ends; a failed write leaves only **Write the Living World**, which retries it with no game master turn. The grown sheet, the Living World lines with it, is written back over the player's own character file, and that character's next Loner game gives the lines to the worldsmith. A character that ships with the game is never rewritten.

The player's own actions, which the game master never sees. Each is a move above the send box:

- **Ask** — the player taps it, types a yes/no question and sends; the game master's next `ask` cites the tags, and the engine rolls the player's words as written, whatever `question` the game master sends. The game master never declines it; an unasked question is dropped at the end of the turn.
- **Inspire** — rolls the inspiration tables, as `roll_inspiration` does; a game master turn follows and reads the three words into the scene. It is offered while the scene is open and no conflict is.
- **Take the breather** — the aim of a Quiet scene; in the breather it is the only move, and the send box takes nothing else.
- **Move on** — closes the scene as `moved_on` and rolls the transition, as `close_scene` does; the worldsmith writes what comes next at once, and neither its intent nor the arrival quotes the words of the scene left behind. No arrival narration reads the player's last words: it tells the new scene's frame and situation, since the closing turn told the leaving. It is not offered during a Harm & Luck conflict, nor in a Quiet scene before the player has played a turn there, so moving on never refills luck with no fiction.
- **Fight** (one chip; with more than one living character here who is not in the party or already fought, it opens a pick of whom to fight) — opens a Harm & Luck conflict with them, refilling luck as the SRD's conflict start does; the game master's next `ask` is the first exchange.
- **Break away** (in place of **Move on** while a conflict is open) — ends the conflict; the game master names the cost next turn.
- **End** — asks the ending decision (**End it** or **Play on**), so one tap never ends the game; **End it** asks what the protagonist learned.
- **End it**, **Play on** and **Write the Living World**.

## Deviations in this repo

Every divergence between `src/rulehall/engines/loner4e/` and the official rules, with the reason it stands. Nothing diverges silently: a rule not listed here is implemented as printed. The SRD's optional tools are not deviations by their own text: the Adventure Maker and the 5W+H setup are offered for when you want structure and stay authoring-time — scenarios here are authored ahead of play; deciding when to ask the Oracle is the player-seat judgment — the game master holds that seat. Challenge Tracks says "skip this section entirely" and Leverage says "skip this section", so neither is played; without Leverage the SRD says to "narrate Yes, and... results immediately as bonuses". The Status Track also says "skip this section" and is not played: a defeat is told in the fiction and may leave a `condition`. Appendix C, *Loner Diceless*, is by its own words a variant that removes dice — an alternative game, not a rule of this one. Appendix D, *Step Dice Oracle*, is an optional variant and is not played; Appendices E and F restate the core rules.

1. **The party.** The solo SRD has no party; here `join_party` and `leave_party` mark who travels with the player, and the party follows into the next scene. Members never ask the oracle and never roll: their help is a tag of theirs cited in `helps`.
2. **The game master is an interpreter.** The player may ask the oracle directly; the game master asks on the player's behalf when an action needs a question, cites the tags and reads the answer. Code rolls every die. This adapts 4e's "With a Game Master" line, where the players consult the oracle and the GM interprets.
3. **The Living World is carried as lines.** It is told as the closing card and stored with the grown character; that character's next Loner game gives it to the worldsmith. NPC state in the ended game is not rewritten.
4. **A second 6 after a Meanwhile reads as Dramatic.** The follow-up roll (reading 41) would make a second 6 another Meanwhile; it reads as Dramatic, so a Meanwhile never chains another.
5. **Pack counters and re-rolls are played in words and tags.** AP03 Hero Points, AP04 Heat, AP07 Action Points, AP08 Cover and AP11 Wanted Levels live in the story and in tags; code keeps no count of them and cannot re-roll an answer. A tool for each pack's counter would give the game master tools that one pack uses.
6. **AP01's "No, but" gives no luck back.** AP01 says a spell that fails on a No, but does not expend the Luck. The game master pays the cost with `spend_luck` before the `ask` that decides the spell, so the luck is gone before the answer, and code refunds none.

## Readings where the SRD is open

1. **Tag netting.** `sign(helps − hinders)` gives one extra die at most (the alley example). `Untrained` is a reserved hindrance for expertise the protagonist lacks. Any cited tag may help or hinder: the SRD's *Walks on a Crutch* ("the same Frailty that hinders in one scene may help in another") shows that a side is the fiction's, and a side check refused a harmless choice more often than it caught an error.
2. **Relevance is the game master's.** Code checks only that each cited tag exists on something known here (the sheet, someone met, the scene's details, `Untrained`) and is cited once. The card shows the cited tags.
3. **Who a question favours.** Action questions are asked from the protagonist's side; the ally question code rolls itself, the dead-end question and the run-its-course question, asked with no tags, are neutral.
4. **The scene's goal.** The worldsmith writes it; `drive(actor_id: scene)` rewrites it when the player's first words set another aim, or after "Changes the goal". The game master resolves the player's action and closes the scene only when that resolves, blocks or abandons the goal; leaving is the player's **Move on**.
5. **The closing test.** The game master closes with the SRD's reasons, which `close_scene` names (resolved, blocked, abandoned), asks "Has this scene run its course?" as the SRD's last resort, or the player picks **Move on**; no prompt repeats the test, which would push early closes. There is no question quota: "six to eight questions" is a first session, not a scene.
6. **Off-screen changes.** Every Meanwhile update is told on the cutaway card; a character elsewhere cannot be cited.
7. **The twist's actions.** "Ends the scene" closes the scene through the twist's own question; "Changes the goal" is `drive` on the scene; "Alters the location" is a scene `detail`.
8. **Several opponents.** Each keeps its own pool (the SRD's option 2); a group is one character (option 1) with 6 luck, as every character has. An opponent at 0 leaves the conflict while others fight on.
9. **The framing question is rules text.** Code cannot tell a question about what is already true from an action question.
10. **Relationship tags live on the NPC.**
11. **Luck refills at a conflict's start and end**, as the SRD says. Only a pack that spends luck outside a conflict (such as an AP01 spell) leaves it short there; that luck refills when a Quiet scene opens, and in any other pack a Quiet scene finds luck full. There is no rest tool.
12. **No open-threads list.** The worldsmith keeps the threads in `arc`, and the history holds the rest.
13. **No pause between exchanges.** The SRD asks nothing of the player there: the game master interprets each exchange, one per turn, and the player's next words press on, change tack or break away. Exchanges are not recorded in SETTLED THIS SCENE: an exchange is not an established fact.
14. **The end order:** the player's decision, the growth, the Living World, the end of the game, the sheet written back.
15. **Twist intensity is the game master's judgment**, stated in one line of the twist note: land it hard when the scene shows pressure, as a shift when it has been clean.
16. **The Twist Counter rests while a Harm & Luck conflict is open**, for every question, as the SRD says it "does not apply during Harm & Luck conflicts". Doubles on the questions code rolls itself count too.
17. **When to ask is rules text, not a code gate.** Code cannot tell an obvious try from an uncertain one.
18. **The grown sheet keeps what play did to it.** The SRD's growth says nothing of clearing conditions or restoring lost tags ("The world doesn't reset between sessions"), so only luck refills.
19. **A comma inside a tag reads as "and".** Tags are listed with commas, so a comma would split a tag when it is cited: code writes "No Flag, No Name" as "No Flag and No Name", in a model's answer, a typed trait and an authored file alike. A tag past 48 characters is refused, and the model's one retry names it shorter.
20. **"On a No, they hold."** Code cannot tell an ally from the power by tags, so the Meanwhile answer names the two apart: `power` for whoever holds power, `ally` for the NPC most affected, and code refuses an `ally` on a No.
21. **A second dead end counts per scene.** The SRD's second dead end is on "the same investigation thread". Code keeps no thread, so the scene stands in for it: `ask` refuses the dead-end question once SETTLED THIS SCENE holds it, and the refusal says to close the scene as `blocked`, the SRD's "reframe entirely".
22. **The end is picked, not typed.** The SRD leaves the end to the player's judgment. The ending decision takes **End it** or **Play on** only, so no reading of the player's words ends the game, and the growth is asked apart ("What did {name} learn?").
23. **A pause holds the narration.** While the player's decision waits, nothing is narrated; the turn that answers it tells the earlier results with the settled one, so no prose preempts the choice. A `direct` sent with the pausing call is kept and told once, on that turn. The game master of the answering turn reads the held results as fixed and already told, with their outcome words, and interprets only the player's answer.
24. **A lost exchange costs luck only.** Inside Harm & Luck the harm is luck; a defeat may leave a `condition`, the SRD's tag for a consequence of lasting weight. `rules.md` says so and code does not enforce it: in an earlier test a condition written after each exchange became a home-made harm track.
25. **Handed-over gear on the grown sheet.** Code cannot tell from the story that gear changed hands, so the grown sheet keeps whatever `change_tags` never dropped. The growth note asks the game master to drop, with `change_tags` `lost`, any gear the story gave away or lost; a miss stays on the sheet. This is accepted as a model limit.
26. **A Quiet scene lasts.** The SRD's Quiet scene is "a pause to recover, reflect, or make plans" where the protagonist has initiative; a game master that closed it as resolved on its first answer made one-question scenes. `close_scene` refuses `resolved` and `turning_point` on the player's first turn in a Quiet scene; a later turn or the player's **Move on** ends it. In the round 3 test the game master also cut every Quiet scene short with a `turning_point` on its first turn: the SRD's turning point is the moment "pressure builds within a Quiet Scene and the fiction tips toward urgency", which needs the player to have played there first. A round 3 refusal after an `ask` in the same turn is gone: in the round 4 test it held a Quiet scene through an alarm, a flight and a fight.
27. **The end is the game master's call or the player's.** The signs sit in `end_adventure`'s description and the player always has the **End** move. No code trigger offers the end: an `abandoned` close is common mid-adventure (four in the round 2 test, none an ending), and code cannot tell "no new direction", so a trigger would only add a question. In the round 4 test the game master never proposed the end, even with clear signs, so a `resolved` close's result asks it to call `end_adventure` when the close settles what the protagonist set out to do; in the round 5 test the player stated both goals reached and the game master closed as `abandoned`, so that close carries the same note. It is a note, not a trigger: the game master still judges.
28. **What happened outranks the arc.** The SRD keeps open threads in the player's head; here the worldsmith's `arc` holds them, and in the round 2 test it rewrote settled facts that the master and narrator then followed. The `arc` is pressure and intent only and never restates what happened; the worldsmith reads the scene's SETTLED answers and the last turn's told facts as FIXED, and the master's THE ARC section says SCENE, the sheet and SETTLED THIS SCENE override it. The narrator never reads the arc.
29. **In Harm & Luck every question is an exchange.** The SRD's Harm & Luck asks every question from the protagonist's side and deals damage on each ("Success on those questions deals damage to the opponent; failure deals damage to the Protagonist"), and a conflict ends when a side "withdraws, or the opposition dissolves". So an `ask` with no `opponent_id` is an exchange against the opponent fought last, and the game master ends the conflict with `withdraw` when the protagonist stops fighting (tending a wound, talking a beaten foe down). In the round 3 test a lone-opponent rule turned a tended wound into an exchange, and with two opponents a fighting `ask` with no `opponent_id` cost nothing.
30. **The growth follows the end.** The SRD's growth ("At the end of the adventure, modify your Protagonist") is a new skill, gear or frailty; in play, a lasting mark is a condition. A new skill or frailty on the protagonist is refused until the player picks **End it**, and one is taken; gear comes and goes in play as before.
31. **One Twist Counter, `Loner4eWorld.twist`.** One protagonist, one counter, kept beside the world and shown to the player on the sheet.
32. **Trait labels carry no meaning of their own.** The SRD prints its d66 tables bare; a converted pack's `concept`, `skill`, `frailty` and `gear` labels are exactly what it prints, with no rules meaning attached. The glossary the game master reads (WHAT THE TAGS IN PLAY MEAN) lists only the entries that do carry a `brief`, which the SRD's own tables never do.
33. **The world is emergent, with a premise.** A scenario is a premise, an opening scene and a web of people who may turn up (the SRD's own "Before the Adventure"). Nothing is hidden, the player knows everyone in the cast as the SRD's solo player does, and no twist is planned; the arc is loose pressure (what the opposition wants, the open threads). What is established stands; the oracle decides the rest.
34. **Scenarios are written ahead of play.** 5W+H, the Adventure Maker and the transition meaning lines are not transcribed: the worldsmith frames each scene from the source, the arc and the rolled transition.
35. **Meanwhile is a cutaway, not a scene.** The worldsmith plays both steps (whoever holds power, and the ally or wildcard when the oracle says yes) as tag updates; the player reads what moved on one card and does not play it. The ally question and any twist it rolls reach the cards only; the game master and the narrator read them as "the world moves off screen".
36. **The question text is public.** The player reads every question on its card. No private note is stored.
37. **Code rules the conflict boundary.** A conflict starts at the first exchange against an opponent or on the player's **Fight**, and ends at 0 luck, on `withdraw` or **Break away**, when the last opponent leaves or dies, or when the scene closes; each end refills every pool. While it is open, every question is an exchange (reading 29).
38. **Goal, motive and nemesis are creation steps**, optional ("Leave empty to let play decide"), as the SRD lets them emerge from play; the opening scene offers a goal. The concept is written, and the skills, frailty and gear are typed in the player's own words or picked from the pack tables, which are suggestions (the SRD has the player invent them); a comma in a typed tag reads as "and" (reading 19).
39. **The breather.** A Quiet scene's aim is the player's words on **Take the breather**; the worldsmith frames it as the scene's goal.
40. **The twist table lives in code.** Code keys on "Ends the scene", "Changes the goal" and "Alters the location"; a pack cannot replace the columns.
41. **A Meanwhile is followed by a rolled scene.** The SRD makes the Meanwhile the world's turn and says nothing of the scene after it. Code rolls a follow-up d6 on the same transition table (1-3 Dramatic, 4-5 Quiet; a 6, deviation 4).
42. **The inspiration roll always rolls the adjective.** The SRD makes it optional ("Roll at least a Verb and a Noun. Add an Adjective for more nuance."); `roll_inspiration` rolls all three and the prompt carries the adjective.

## What the AI game master adds

Fields and tools that exist for the app around the rules, not the rules themselves — each earns its keep for a reason the SRD has no need of.

- `packs/srd.json`'s starter tables — the concepts, skills, frailties and gear in that pack are written for this repo under its own licence; Loner 4e publishes no such tables, and the pack holds no SRD text. The twist and transition tables are the SRD's, in `rules.py`. A creation table is the SRD's entries followed by the chosen pack's, so the starter tables are offered beside an Adventure Pack's.
- `known` — every Loner character is known, as the SRD's solo player sees everything, so no name is gated and a Loner scene holds nothing hidden. Loner publishes no `reveal`.
- `alive` and `kill` — the SRD leaves death to narration; the gate needs a flag.
- The scene frame — each scene carries its `goal` and two to four `details` (the SRD's "Framing a Scene"), and the game master's one lifecycle call is `close_scene`; the engine rolls the transition and hands over to the worldsmith or the breather.
- SETTLED THIS SCENE — every answer the oracle gave in this scene, so the game master never asks it again.
- MET, NOT HERE — one line per living cast member who is elsewhere (`Name[id] — concept`), so the game master reuses an id instead of filing a stranger twice (the round 5 test filed Colonel Kolb as a second "delegation woman").
- Moves that take words — **Ask** and **Take the breather** take the player's own words from the send box.
- The Living World carried with the character — the grown sheet is written back over the player's own character file, and WHAT THE PROTAGONIST CARRIES FORWARD gives its lines to the worldsmith in that character's next Loner game.

## Where the rules live

Mechanics are in `src/rulehall/engines/loner4e/`; the scene machinery shared with the other scene engines is `SceneEngine` in `src/rulehall/engines/scenes/`. `rules.py` — not this file — is the transcription of record: the twist table, the inspiration tables, the outcome lines, the fixed questions and the transition ranges.
