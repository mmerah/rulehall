# 24XX

A rules-light SF/adventure system: one skill die from d6 to d12, +d6 when the conditions help,
an ally's own skill die when they help, d4 when hindered, keep the highest, three outcome bands.

## The tools

Every tool makes one change, rolls dice, opens a decision, or ends the turn.

- `reveal`, `enter`, `leave`, `kill`, `leave_party`,
  `change_hindrances`, `gain_item`, `drop_item`, `repair_item`, `spend`, `defend` (break a carried
  item or a ship function so a hit becomes a hindrance; hull armor and the android case break harmlessly), `ship_upgrade` (₡10, once per
  function), and `lose_hold_item` (an item in the hold is lost). The sheet tools take `actor_id`.
- `next_scene` — offer the player the way on at a stopping point; or say that they left this
  place, and the worldsmith writes where they land; or hand the worldsmith a complication to
  bring down on this place. The last two end the turn.
- `roll` — a skill, or the plain d6 when none applies, on the 1–2 / 3–4 / 5+ ladder, with help
  or hindrance when they apply; `helped_by` names a hired member who rolls their own skill die beside the
  actor and carries their own hindrance and stakes; `actor_id` when a hired member acts. `risk` names
  before the dice what the actor suffers in full on a disaster; `deadly` marks when that risk is
  death, so a lethal band is one the player was told about. `defend_with_id` names the gear that
  breaks to spare them instead and `hindrance` what that leaves behind. Gear is checked before
  the roll, so a defense the rules cannot honour never costs a die.
- `ask_world` — one d6, read as trouble now / signs of it / nothing, for a question about the
  world where nobody acts.
- `job` — `find` rolls the SRD's d6 for work (the ₡1 re-roll is `spend`); `take` records the
  terms as agreed under THE JOB; `finish` raises the skill the job called on for the player and
  every living hired member — one the sheet lacks enters at d8 — pays each their own d6 of
  credits, and clears the job.
- `join_party` — someone here travels with the player. With `terms`, the player hires them to
  work: the worldsmith writes their sheet once the turn ends and they join the party.

`take_lead` is a player action: the `succession` decision calls it, and the master never sees it.

The ship has a hold. `ship_here` on the opening and on each next scene tells if the ship is at
the player's place. Each carried item has a gear row with two options: "Stow in the hold" moves
the item to the hold, and "Drop" removes it. Each item in the hold has a row on the Ship panel,
with "Take it" for the player and "Give to X" for each hired member. Each ship function has an
"Upgrade" option. Stow, Take it, Give to and Upgrade are refused when the ship is not here.

## Deviations in this repo

Every divergence between `src/rulehall/engines/twentyfourxx/` and the official rules, and every
choice made where the SRD leaves the table to decide, with the reason each stands. A mechanic
not listed here — the dice ladder, the outcome bands, defense, advancement, invented skills — is
the SRD's exact one; what follows is either a real divergence or a deliberate reading of an open
rule.

1. **The priced gear table is not transcribed.** Buying is play through `gain_item` and `spend`,
   with the SRD's list as the master's vocabulary.
2. **Death is answered with hired-member succession, not a replacement character.** The SRD
   favors promptly introducing a new character; a living hired member takes the lead here
   instead, and the game ends when none remain. Deliberate, unchanged.

Four readings the SRD leaves open are settled without diverging from it: help and hindrance may
both apply to the same roll, since the SRD covers each in its own sentence and never says they are
exclusive; a job is what `job` `take` recorded, so `job` `finish` fires once per job, from the player's
own words that the work is finished; a luck test is one d6 read on trouble now / signs of it /
nothing, the SRD's "interpret the result as you see fit" made concrete; and succession keeps the
new lead's own id, while `leads` marks who the player is, so traces and cards follow the lead.

## What the AI game master adds

Fields and tools that exist for the app around the rules, not the rules themselves.

- `known` and `hidden` — the told-fact gate: no unknown name reaches the narrator.
- `alive`, `kill` and `roll`'s `deadly` — the SRD leaves whether a disaster is one of death to
  the table watching the fiction; the app has no such judge mid-roll, so `deadly` states that
  ruling as data before the dice, and `alive`/`kill` carry out what it decides.
- `next_scene` and the scene's `offered` flag — a stopping point the player may stay at.
- `hindrances` as a plain list on the sheet — the SRD's word for what slows the player down, kept
  as data so the narrator can read it back.
- a sheet on a cast member marks who has dice; the worldsmith writes it on `join_party` with
  `terms`.
