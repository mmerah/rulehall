# 24XX

A rules-light SF/adventure system: one skill die from d6 to d12, +d6 when the conditions help, an ally's own skill die when they help, d4 when hindered, keep the highest, three outcome bands.

## The tools

Every tool makes one change, rolls dice, opens a decision, or ends the turn.

- `reveal`, `enter` (an id with no cast entry files a met stranger named from the id, so a person the player names can be met and hired), `leave` (of a dead body does nothing), `kill`, `leave_party` (a hired member too, when the player lets them go in words; it and **Let go** tell that the member is no longer with the crew, who keeps their sheet and comes back on a new `join_party`; their cast line then says "let go from the crew" and where they were last seen; `leave_party` of someone not with the crew does nothing), `change_hindrances` (a hindrance already carried is skipped; a lost `X` also finds `Brief: X`; a lost one not carried is refused with the list of what is), `gain_item`, `drop_item` (of an item already gone does nothing), `repair_item` (an item in the hold too, while the ship is here), `spend` (with `to_id`, the credits go to the player or a hired member; anyone else, a hire paid up front before the sign-on is written too, simply takes them), `defend` (break a carried item or a ship function so a hit becomes a brief hindrance, written `Brief: …`, which code removes at the next scene; a hindrance already carried is not written twice; hull armor and the android case break harmlessly), `ship_upgrade` (₡10 each, bought in words as gear is, as many per function as the player pays for, each named in `upgrade` and listed on that function in THE SHIP and on the Ship panel), and `lose_hold_item` (an item in the hold is lost). The sheet tools take `actor_id`.
- `next_scene` — offer the player the way on at a stopping point; or say that they left this place, and the worldsmith writes where they land, the place they named (`by_ship` when the crew flies its own ship there, and the arrival is told as a flight; a flight that lands on the `place_id` it left is refused); or test for bad luck here with `complication`, the trouble that would come: code rolls the SRD's d6, and on 1–2 the worldsmith brings that trouble down on this place, on 3–4 the master shows only a sign of it, and on 5–6 nothing comes. The card shows the band, never the trouble. A departure and trouble that comes end the turn. The narrator tells the leaving before the worldsmith writes the crossing, so a failed write says only that the crossing is not written yet and the player moves on again to arrive. The way on and the departure are 24XX's own: the scene family shared with Loner holds no place-based rule.
- `roll` — a skill, or the plain d6 when none applies, on the 1–2 / 3–4 / 5+ ladder, with help or hindrance when they apply (`helped` only for a circumstance the story has set up, never scenery); `helped_by` names a hired member who rolls beside the actor their own die in the `skill` they help with, or in the roll's `skill` when the master names none (a d6 when their sheet lacks it), and shares the actor's `risk`, `harm`, `deadly` and `setback_hurt` unless the master names their own `risk`; they carry their own hindrance and stakes, so a helper's `harm` setback writes the same brief as the actor's; `actor_id` when a hired member acts. `risk` names before the dice what the actor suffers in full on a disaster, in 100 characters at most (a longer one is refused, and the model's one retry names it shorter), and with `harm` it names the injury itself, since it becomes the hindrance; `deadly` marks when that risk is death; with `harm`, `setback_hurt` names the lesser hurt a setback leaves, one step down from `risk`. `defend` names the gear the player named before the roll to break and spare them instead (`item_id`), and what that leaves behind (`hindrance`), so a hindrance never travels without its item. The pool and the gear are checked first, so a roll the rules cannot honour never costs a die. Then, unless `committed` is set, nothing rolls: the `risk` decision shows the player the dice, the stakes, whether they hurt (`harm`) or are deadly, the actor's hindrances, more than one bulky item, and a pool that cannot reach 5. **Commit** is `roll` again with `committed`, so it rolls at once; a text answer is a revision the master rules on. `harm` marks a risk that hurts the body. A disaster on a `harm` roll that no gear softens writes the `risk` on the sheet as a hindrance (once: a hindrance already carried is not written again). After a disaster on a `harm` roll, or a disaster or setback on a `deadly` roll, that lands on the actor or the helper with unbroken gear and no `defend`, the `defence` decision withholds the result, for the actor first and then the helper: break any unbroken carried item to turn the hit into a brief hindrance (the `setback_hurt`, or `Close call` when the risk was death), or take it; every such hit offers it, a second in the same scene too. Either choice lands the result, and the master reads it on that turn. A `harm` setback writes the `setback_hurt` on the sheet as a brief hindrance, never the full risk, which code removes at the next scene, and a `defend` named for it leaves the gear whole with a trace, since the setback is already the lesser consequence. A `deadly` setback writes `Maimed` on the sheet, and its defence prompt says the actor is maimed; the master gives the wound itself its own hindrance, and an actor already maimed gets no second maim.
- `job` — three plain verbs. `find` rolls the SRD's d6 for work and tells the master what to offer (two jobs to pick from in words; one job that seems off; on nothing, no work unless the crew takes a job that leaves them owing somebody; the master writes the catch into the terms), and the result goes into SETTLED THIS SCENE as "Work at …"; while a job is open, `find` is refused with a pointer to `finish`. After a find, the player has the **Pay ₡1 and look again** move (while they hold ₡1), which re-rolls, until a job is taken, and a game master turn follows that tells the new offer. `take` with no job open is refused until a `find` since the last take, in any scene, so the SRD's d6 decides the offer (the round 6 test took three jobs with no find); it sets the terms as given under THE JOB, with no credit figure, since pay is the d6 at the end, and goes into SETTLED THIS SCENE as "Job taken"; a `take` while a job is open replaces its terms, keeping its rolled work, and closes nothing. `finish` is refused until a roll has been played since the job was taken, so the work is played, not narrated, and ignores stray `terms` or `where`; it raises the skill the job called on for every living hired member — one the sheet lacks enters at d8 — pays every operator their own d6 of credits and clears the job; the master is told that pay is the crew's cut for the work done, a failed job's too. The player's own raise is owed from then: the `raise` decision opens when the turn ends, as succession does, so the master plays the rest of the player's words first, and it opens again at each turn's end until the raise is taken.
- `raise_skill` — the player's own raise after a job: an option of the `raise` decision, or the master's call when the player names it in words. Refused unless a raise is owed.
- `bring_in` — the lead is dead and no hired member lives: the player describes who joins the crew; the worldsmith picks the new operator's specialty, origin and their options from those words, and the engine builds the sheet through the creation steps the character page uses (the specialty's skills and kit, the origin's increases, traits or body, the starting kit and ₡2). They lead, and join in the scene where the lead died: the worldsmith is told so, and the master's next turn gets a note that they are here. After any succession, the narrator's SCENE and the worldsmith's THE SCENE NOW say who the player is now and that only their sheet's hindrances describe their body, so the dead lead's wounds never pass to them. The death is answered once the turn ends, so the master reads it first; a failed write asks again at once.
- `join_party` — someone here travels with the player. An id with no cast entry files a stranger named from the id, who enters first, so the master can hire someone the story just brought in. With `terms`, the player hires them to work: once the turn ends the worldsmith picks their creation choices, the engine builds their sheet as for `bring_in`, and they join the party.

`take_lead` and `defend_hit` are player actions: the `succession` and `defence` decisions call them, and the master never sees them. `find_again` is the **Pay ₡1 and look again** move, offered while a find stands and no job is taken. The **Let go** option on a hired member's Party rows calls `leave_party`, which is also a master tool. `move_on` is the player action behind **Move on**: the player's words become the turn, and the master plays the leaving.

The ship has a hold. The ship is docked at a location: the opening's `ship_at`, or the opening's own location when that is empty. It stays there until the crew flies it away with `next_scene` `by_ship`, which docks it where they land, so the ship is here whenever the scene's location is the one it is docked at. Code owns it: the worldsmith never says where the ship is after the opening. Each carried item has a gear row with two options: "Stow in the hold" moves the item to the hold, and "Drop" removes it. Each item in the hold has a row on the Ship panel, with "Take it" for the player and "Give to X" for each hired member. Stow, Take it and Give to are refused when the ship is not here. The page has no upgrade option: the player buys an upgrade in words, and the master calls `ship_upgrade`.

## Deviations in this repo

Every divergence between `src/rulehall/engines/twentyfourxx/` and the official rules, with the reason each stands. A mechanic not listed here is the SRD's own or a reading of an open rule, listed after.

1. **A helper without a sheet shares no risk.** The SRD has a helping ally share the risk; a party member who is not hired helps through `helped` only, because they have no sheet to carry the hit.
2. **No ship emergency structure.** In the SRD each crew member picks a ship function to attempt; with one player and an NPC crew that means little, so an emergency is a plain roll and the player names the function in their words.
3. **The after-roll defence covers `harm` and `deadly` hits only, with carried items only.** A loss, a cost or an alarm is not a hit; a ship function breaks for defence only through `defend`, the tool the master calls when the player names it.

Readings the SRD leaves open are settled without diverging from it: the commit pause has one escape, since the master may set `committed` itself when the player's own words for this action name this danger and accept it ("I jump even if I fall"), and a bare action never does; code writes the SRD's fixed consequences, a `deadly` setback `Maimed` ("If risking death, you're maimed") and an undefended `harm` disaster its `risk` as a lasting hindrance ("Suffer the full risk"), and a `defend` named for a `harm` setback leaves the gear whole, since the setback is already the lesser consequence; a failed job pays and raises too, `finish` closing it as a done one, since the SRD pays and advances "after a job", and the pay is told as the crew's cut for the work done; help and hindrance may both apply to the same roll, since the SRD covers each in its own sentence and never says they are exclusive; a job is what `job` `take` recorded, and `job` `finish` fires once per job, when the job is over in the story (the work its terms name is done and handed over, or the job has failed for good), never on the player's word alone, since the SRD pays and advances "after a job"; a find waits for the open job's `finish`, while a take amends the open job's terms; a find's ₡1 re-roll lasts until a job is taken; the master names a hired member's raise, while the player picks their own; a broken item stays broken, never destroyed, until someone repairs it ("Broken gear is useless until repaired"); a death with a hired member alive is answered by that member taking the lead, and with none by a new operator the player describes ("favor inclusion over realism"), whose creation choices the worldsmith makes from the player's words, so the game never ends on a death; a hired member is made the same way, since the SRD has one creation for every character; and succession gives the new lead the player's id and files the dead lead under the slug of their name, so every view shows the new lead as the player and the dead as someone else; the dead lead's gear goes to the ship's hold, where the crew can take it back, and their credits go to the new lead, the SRD naming no rule for them; a brief hindrance lasts until the next scene, the SRD saying only "brief"; a second maim adds nothing, since the SRD names one maim and no escalation; a lesser hurt the master leaves unnamed is written `Minor hurt`, and a deadly hit that gear softens `Close call`, so code never writes a blank; the bad-luck test runs when the master calls it, since the SRD says "as needed"; and what the player has met and learned (`known`, the briefs) stays after a succession, as a table's player keeps what they learned: the master plays in the fiction what the new lead has not yet heard.

## What the AI game master adds

Fields and tools that exist for the app around the rules, not the rules themselves.

- `known` and `hidden` — the told-fact gate: no hidden name reaches the narrator. A name that a tool text or the narration tells the player makes that cast member met; someone hidden in the scene stays unnamed until `reveal`.
- `alive`, `kill` and `roll`'s `deadly` — the SRD leaves whether a disaster is one of death to the table watching the fiction; the app has no such judge mid-roll, so `deadly` states that ruling as data before the dice, and `alive`/`kill` carry out what it decides.
- `next_scene` with nothing set, and **Move on** always on the page — a stopping point the player may stay at, and a way on the player can always take in their own words.
- `hindrances` as a plain list on the sheet — the SRD's word for what slows the player down, kept as data so the narrator can read it back.
- a sheet on a cast member marks who has dice; the worldsmith writes it on `join_party` with `terms`.
- cast ids — code files each worldsmith cast entry under the slug of its name; the guidance keeps the crew's ship out of the cast.
