# 24XX

A rules-light SF/adventure system: one skill die from d6 to d12, +d6 when the conditions help, an ally's own skill die when they help, d4 when hindered, keep the highest, three outcome bands.

## Official sources

- SRD (v1.4, the text this repo's engine was built from): <https://24xx-srd.carrd.co/>
- Official downloads — plain text, PDF, layout templates, vector logo (v1.41 at time of writing): <https://jasontocci.itch.io/24xx>
- Explanatory mirror of a slightly different revision, with `rules/`, `characters/`, `gear/`, `details/` and `gm-page/` pages: <https://keeper.farirpgs.com/resources/jason-tocci/24xx/>
- The 200+ hacks at <https://itch.io/c/1204990/24xx> are separately licensed and are not drop-in packs.

## The tools

The master's tools are the `@tool` methods of `src/rulehall/engines/twentyfourxx/engine.py` and its bases. Their docstrings are exactly what the model reads.

## Player actions

`take_lead` and `defend_hit` are player actions: the `succession` and `defence` decisions call them, and the master never sees them. `find_again` is the **Pay ₡1 and look again** move, offered while a find stands and no job is taken. The **Let go** option on a hired member's Party rows calls `leave_party`, which is also a master tool. `move_on` is the player action behind **Move on**: the player's words become the turn, and the master plays the leaving.

The ship is docked at a location: the opening's `ship_at`, or the opening's own location when that is empty. It stays there until the crew flies it away with `next_scene` `by_ship`, which docks it where they land, so the ship is here whenever the scene's location is the one it is docked at. Code owns it: the worldsmith never says where the ship is after the opening. Each carried item has a gear row with two options: "Stow in the hold" moves the item to the hold, and "Drop" removes it. Each item in the hold has a row on the Ship panel, with "Take it" for the player and "Give to X" for each hired member. Stow, Take it and Give to are refused when the ship is not here. The page has no upgrade option: the player buys an upgrade in words, and the master calls `ship_upgrade`.

## Deviations in this repo

Every divergence between `src/rulehall/engines/twentyfourxx/` and the official rules, with the reason each stands. A mechanic not listed here is the SRD's own or a reading of an open rule, listed after.

1. **A helper without a sheet shares no risk.** The SRD has a helping ally share the risk; a party member who is not hired helps through `helped` only, because they have no sheet to carry the hit. A hired helper shares the roller's risk and cannot take a different one.
2. **No ship emergency structure.** In the SRD each crew member picks a ship function to attempt; with one player and an NPC crew that means little, so an emergency is a plain roll and the player names the function in their words.
3. **The defence covers `harm` and `deadly` hits only, and the player picks the gear after the roll.** A loss, a cost or an alarm is not a hit. The `defence` decision offers the break once the dice show a hit: every unbroken carried item, and each ship function while the ship is here.

Readings the SRD leaves open are settled without diverging from it: the commit pause has one escape, since the master may set `committed` itself when the player's own words for this action name this danger and accept it ("I jump even if I fall"), and a bare action never does; code writes the SRD's fixed consequences, a `deadly` setback `Maimed` ("If risking death, you're maimed") and an undefended `harm` disaster its `risk` as a lasting hindrance ("Suffer the full risk"), and a `harm` setback offers no defence, since the setback is already the lesser consequence; a failed job pays and raises too, `finish` closing it as a done one, since the SRD pays and advances "after a job", and the pay is told as the crew's cut for the work done; help and hindrance may both apply to the same roll, since the SRD covers each in its own sentence and never says they are exclusive; a job is what `job` `take` recorded, and `job` `finish` fires once per job, when the job is over in the story (the work its terms name is done and handed over, or the job has failed for good), never on the player's word alone, since the SRD pays and advances "after a job"; a find waits for the open job's `finish`, while a take amends the open job's terms; a find's ₡1 re-roll lasts until a job is taken; the master names a hired member's raise, while the player picks their own; a broken item stays broken, never destroyed, until someone repairs it ("Broken gear is useless until repaired"); a death with a hired member alive is answered by that member taking the lead, and with none by a new operator the player describes ("favor inclusion over realism"), whose creation choices the worldsmith makes from the player's words, so the game never ends on a death; a hired member is made the same way, since the SRD has one creation for every character; and succession gives the new lead the player's id and files the dead lead under the slug of their name, so every view shows the new lead as the player and the dead as someone else; the dead lead's gear goes to the ship's hold, where the crew can take it back, and their credits go to the new lead, the SRD naming no rule for them; a brief hindrance lasts until the next scene, the SRD saying only "brief"; a second maim adds nothing, since the SRD names one maim and no escalation; a lesser hurt the master leaves unnamed is written `Minor hurt`, and a deadly hit that gear softens `Close call`, so code never writes a blank; the bad-luck test runs when the master calls it, since the SRD says "as needed"; and what the player has met and learned (`known`, the briefs) stays after a succession, as a table's player keeps what they learned: the master plays in the fiction what the new lead has not yet heard.

## What the AI game master adds

Fields and tools that exist for the app around the rules, not the rules themselves.

- `known` and `hidden` — the told-fact gate: no hidden name reaches the narrator. A name that a tool text or the narration tells the player makes that cast member met; someone hidden in the scene stays unnamed until `reveal`.
- `alive`, `kill` and `roll`'s `deadly` — the SRD leaves whether a disaster is one of death to the table watching the fiction; the app has no such judge mid-roll, so `deadly` states that ruling as data before the dice, and `alive`/`kill` carry out what it decides.
- `next_scene` with nothing set, and **Move on** always on the page — a stopping point the player may stay at, and a way on the player can always take in their own words.
- `hindrances` as a plain list on the sheet — the SRD's word for what slows the player down, kept as data so the narrator can read it back.
- a sheet on a cast member marks who has dice; the worldsmith writes it on `join_party` with `terms`.
- cast ids — code files each worldsmith cast entry under the slug of its name; the guidance keeps the crew's ship out of the cast.
